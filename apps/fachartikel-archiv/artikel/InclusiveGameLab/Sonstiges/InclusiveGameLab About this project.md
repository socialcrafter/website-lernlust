# InclusiveGameLab About this project

InclusiveGameLab was funded by the Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space (BMFTR). The project was a cooperation between the University of Applied Science (TH Köln) and the Department of Youth Media Culture of North Rhine-Westphalia (fjmk).

The InclusiveGameLab went from September 2024 until February 2026 und aimed at fostering inclusive approaches to digital games. Since accessibility is such a complex topic, it is easy to either lose the overview or to not know where to search for information. Thus it was our objective to make this information more accessible by splitting the contents.

To reach this goal, we used the following approaches:

1\) First we did thorough research to understand the status quo of accessibility and inclusion in research, teaching and the gaming industry.

We collected articles concerning this topic that were published between the early 80s and 2025, took a look at study paths from around the world to pinpoint whether accessibility as a topic is a pivotal factor in the curricula and checked games to gather data concerning current practices in the field of accessibility in gaming.

2\) Next we founded an online network for German-speaking gamers, experts and other people from interdisciplinary backgrounds with and without disabilities. Such platforms were already existent for English-speaking people, for example the IGDA Game Accessibility Special Interest Group but there was no platform for German-speakers interested in this topic.

The establishment of a dedicated network was important for them, as not everybody speaks English which acted as a barrier and it is easier this way to share information specific for a country, for example concerning events or legislation.

If you are German-speaking and interested in joining the network, you can get to our Discord server by clicking this link: [[https://discord.gg/BjUAF3cMeW]{.underline}](https://discord.gg/BjUAF3cMeW)

3\) Finally, and maybe most importantly, we wanted to do our part in making the gaming industry as well as the field of media education more inclusive and educate game developers as well as media education professionals about accessibility. For this reason we developed so-called Open Educational Resources (OER). As the name implies, these are openly available educational materials which are not only free to use for everybody, but can also be changed, updated and passed on.

The material contains lectures, texts, videos, statements and Power Point presentations, which are all free to use for anyone interested as well as educators. Additionally, there is a comprehensive collection of pictures, which is available on Wikimedia Commons under the tag "Inclusive Game Lab" (see attached document concerning pictures).

# What are Open Educational Resources (OER)?

According to UNESCO, Open Educational Resources are "learning, teaching and research materials in any format and medium that reside in the public domain or are under copyright that have been released under an open license, that permit no-cost access, re-use, re-purpose, adaptation and redistribution by others." (see [[https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-open-educational-resources-oer]{.underline}](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-open-educational-resources-oer))

Further information about OER licences can be found at: [[https://unevoc.unesco.org/home/Open+Licensing+of+Educational+Resources]{.underline}](https://unevoc.unesco.org/home/Open+Licensing+of+Educational+Resources)

# Who can use it and where?

Everything you find in the material can be used for free by everyone and in every context, as well as be changed and shared. Whether you are a teacher, media educator, (future) game developer or simply curious to learn more about accessibility and inclusion in gaming - these resources are just right for you!

The material is licenced under: CC-BY-SA 4.0.\
That means: You can download the material for free, use, change and redistribute it as long as you name our project and use the same licence.

# How are these OER structured?

There are two collections, each with further sub-collections:

In the top collection, there is the file you are currently reading.

In the same collection, there are sub-collections containing:

1.  The lectures for game development in English: This includes content for different disciplines, specifically game design, game art, game programming and sound design. These are separated into three difficulty levels which consider the effort as well as the expected impact/usefulness (for gamers with disabilities). The lectures for all disciplines come with a PowerPoint presentation.

2.  The lectures for media education in German: These are additionally available in plain language. Every lecture comes with a PowerPoint presentation. Furthermore, there is a collection of methods for inclusive pedagogical work.

The lectures have been developed to impart knowledge about a series of different topics which are all connected to accessibility and inclusion in gaming. We decided to provide the material for game developers in English and the material for media educators in German. The respective resources are structured differently to consider the different needs of people with different job functions:

- Game development: The OER for game developers are separated into three different "difficulty levels" (Basic, Intermediate and Expert).

> o On the basic level you find solutions that are relatively easy and affordable to implement while the topics for Intermediate and Expert cover more complex and resource-consuming options that require prior knowledge.
>
> o Furthermore, the contents of every difficulty level are assigned different core divisions of game development - graphics, design, programming and sound - so that users of these resources can easily find the information that is relevant to them. Contents that are relevant for all professionals in the gaming industry can be found in the collection "For all disciplines".

- Media Education: The lectures cover different thematically related media educational areas - this makes them interesting for persons who grapple with the topic gaming for the first time, as well as experts. The lectures are also translated into plain language and can be found in the respective sub-collection. Additionally, there is an extensive collection of pedagogical methods which supports implementation of the learned contents in practical pedagogic work.

For every lecture, there is a script, a PowerPoint presentation and a video recording.
