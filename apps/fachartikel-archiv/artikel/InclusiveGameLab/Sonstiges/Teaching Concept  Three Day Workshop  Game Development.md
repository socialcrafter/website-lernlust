# Teaching Concept  Three Day Workshop  Game Development

InclusiveGameLab, CC-BY-SA 4.0

Teaching Concept:

Three Day Workshop -- Game Development

# Description:

This is a three-day workshop which offers a theoretical foundation, but then focuses more on practical application of accessibility-related knowledge. The workshop is suited for beginner, intermediate, and expert level students and game developers alike.

As is the case with the other workshop, its content can also be split in smaller learning units and across an entire semester.

# General Information

- Title: Game Accessibility -- First Steps

- Number of days: 3

- Hours per day: 6-7

- Maximum number of participants: N/A

- Brief course description: Improving access to games is one of the core challenges the industry is facing at the moment. With an estimated 1.3. Billion disabled people worldwide, an aging population and numerous legal requirements, it's important to consider the topic early. But how and where do you even start? We're going to guide you through your first steps on this journey and provide useful information on universal design, accessibility options, working with consultants, and inclusive marketing, among other topics. Please bring your laptops and game projects (concepts or prototypes) you previously worked on.

# Content

## Day 1

- Short input: What is a Disability? (\~25 min)

- Discussion: Disabilities -- What did you already know? What did you learn?\
  (\~15 min)

- First task: Check the building and the surrounding neighborhood for environmental barriers, take photos, highlight the barrier (\~30 min)

- Discussion: Share your findings (\~15 min)

- Short input: Most Common Barriers in Games (\~25 min)

- Discussion: Which barriers were you already aware of? Which were missing from our examples, but are as important to highlight? (\~10-15 min)

**Break: 30-45 minutes**

- Short play session: Game Over (5 min)

  - One student is asked to play the game in front of the class.

  - The game illustrates common barriers really well and in a fun way. This is a good approach to reactivate and refocus the students after a longer break.

- Short input: Universal Design (\~ 20 min)

- Short input: Accessibility vs. Difficulty (\~ 20 min)

- Play session and discussion: Celeste (\~ 20 min)

**Break: 10 minutes**

- Task for the remaining day/homework: Students are asked to find best practice examples for accessible games and prepare a short presentation for the final day, individually or in small groups.

## Day 2 

- Homework: Students share results (\~30 min)

- Discussion of results (\~15-20 min)

- Play session: A selection of good practice examples is offered to the students. They are asked to play the games thoroughly and take note of accessible design as well as useful features (\~ 60 min)

- Discussion of results (\~15-20 min)

**Break: 10 minutes**

- Group work: revising old game projects

  - Students are asked to revisit their old game projects and check them for unintentional barriers.

  - They are then tasked with removing these barriers: either by retrofitting accessibility features or by redesigning their games from the ground up.

OR

- Students work in groups to develop concepts for games that teach other aspiring game developers about unintentional barriers, accessibility, and universal design. Show "Game Over!" again as a reference and good practice example.

## Day 3 

- Q&A: group projects

- Short input: Accessibility Resources: Guidelines, Websites, Communities\
  (\~ 20 min)

- Short input: How to Work with Accessibility Consultants (\~ 20 min)

- Students proceed with their group projects

**BREAK (30-45 min)**

- Students proceed with their group projects and prepare short presentations

- Students present their results (\~ 45 min)

- Q&A (open end)
